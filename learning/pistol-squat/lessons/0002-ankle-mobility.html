Ankle mobility

Mission: 3 clean, unassisted pistols on each leg. Today's limiter: a narrow, heels-down deep squat isn't there yet.

Today's win: measure your knee-to-wall distance on each leg and text me the two numbers. Then do one round of knee-over-toe pulses. Five minutes.

Why ankles

At the bottom of a pistol your knee has to travel well past your toes with the heel down. If the ankle can't bend that far (dorsiflexion), you fall backward or fold forward. That's the big lean you've noticed. GMB names ankle dorsiflexion as the most common pistol limiter.[1]

1 · Measure: knee-to-wall test

  1. Barefoot, face a wall in a half-kneeling lunge, front foot pointing straight at the wall.
  2. Drive the front knee forward to touch the wall, heel down, knee over the middle toes.
  3. Slide the foot back until you can only just touch. Measure big toe to wall in inches. Both legs.[2]
  4. Retest weekly, same setup. A change of about ¾ in (1.9 cm) is real; smaller is measurement noise.[2]

Rough target: about 4 in (10 cm) or more. That's common field guidance for good dorsiflexion; under about 2½ in (6 cm) is restricted.[3] Some sources use 5 in.[2] Comparing your two legs matters more than any single number.

Fits your short-session habit: do the pulses and squat hold on a tower visit, calf stretches whenever you stand up from the desk.

3 · Temporary heel lift

For pistol practice (Lesson 1 ladder), stand with your heel on a board or small plate, about 1 in high. Raising the heel reduces how far the ankle must bend and cuts forward trunk lean in squats.[5][6] It's a crutch, not the goal: as your knee-to-wall number climbs, step down to a thinner lift, then none. Log reps on a lift as "heel lift" in the variation.
