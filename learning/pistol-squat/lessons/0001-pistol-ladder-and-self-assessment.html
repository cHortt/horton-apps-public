The pistol ladder and how to self-assess

Mission: 3 clean, unassisted pistols on each leg. Today: level 1 (assisted, shaky).

Today's win: on your next tower visit, hold the upright and do 3 slow assisted pistols per leg, then 1 minute of the ankle drill. That's the whole plan in miniature.

What counts as a clean pistol

  1. Stand on one leg, the other leg straight out in front, foot off the floor.[1]
  2. Down under control (about 3 seconds), knee tracking over your toes, heel down, as low as you can.[1]
  3. Up to a straight knee. Free foot never touches down. Some forward lean and rounding at the bottom is normal.[1]

The bottom is the hardest part, not the descent[2], which matches what you feel. Your three limiters: bottom strength, ankle mobility (the big forward lean), and free-leg control/balance.

The ladder

Practitioners converge on supported pistols with less and less help, box pistols at falling height, then a counterweight.[3][1]

LevelVariationHow
1Assisted pistolBoth hands on the tower upright, full depth, pull only as much as you need.
2Light-assist pistolOne hand or fingertips; help only at the very bottom.
3High box pistolSit to a bench about knee height, pause, stand without rocking.
4Low box pistolSame, lowering the box a notch (a book or cushion) each time you pass.
5Counterweight pistolFull depth holding a light dumbbell out in front at arm's length; it shifts balance forward and eases the ankle demand.[4]
6Free pistolThe goal: 3 clean reps per leg.

You can work two rungs side by side (say level 2 and level 3); log the highest you pass.

How to self-assess

  • Pass a rung: 3 clean reps per leg on 2 separate days. Then move up.
  • Not clean: heel lifts, knee caves in, free foot touches, or you bounce/rock up. Stay put.
  • Use the weaker leg to set the level for both.
  • Diagnose the lean: if heels lift in a deep two-leg squat, it's ankles; do the drill.[2]

Check yourself

1. You hit 3 clean high-box reps per leg on two separate days. What now?

2. Your heels lift in a deep two-leg squat. What is the likely limiter?

3. Left leg passes level 3, right does not. Which level do you train?

4. Your free foot brushes the floor at the bottom. How do you count it?
