Grease the groove: your first two weeks

Mission: 20 strict pull-ups, non-stop. Today: 8.

Today's win: do your first groove set of 4 strict pull-ups, then log it. That's the whole plan in miniature.

The idea in one breath

Pull-ups get better when you practise them often and fresh, not when you grind them to exhaustion. Pavel Tsatsouline calls this "greasing the groove": many sub-maximal sets spread through the day, never to failure, so you're "training as often as possible while being as fresh as possible."[1] Small sets need little recovery, which is why you can repeat them many times a day.[2] In Pavel's article, a 60-year-old who did chin-ups every time he went to the basement went on to do 20.[1]

That fits you well: each set takes about 30 seconds, and you don't have to change clothes for it.

Rules for a groove set

  • Strict form: start from a dead hang, get your chin over the bar, and don't kip. Armstrong says quality matters more than numbers.[3]
  • Stop each set while every rep still moves at the same speed. If a rep slows down, the set was too big.
  • Stay well away from failure. Pavel warns that going to exhaustion burns you out.[1]
  • If your elbows or shoulders hurt, take 2 days off and then go back to smaller sets.
  • Missed a day? Don't make it up. Just do the next day as planned.

Why there's a test: one trainee found GTG alone stalled him, and regular max tests plus harder days helped him break through.[4] Lesson 2 will add Armstrong-style structure after your Oct 28 test.
